# MOTIONX outreach — research-thesis framework

Facts are not personalization. Every qualified lead must have a **written thesis**
in this structure before a draft exists. If the public evidence cannot answer the
ten thesis questions in SKILL.md ("The account thesis"), reject the lead.

A thesis is stored as part of the review packet in
`~/.motionx-outreach/review-packets/` — never in the git repo.

**Research depth ≠ email content (v2.2).** This thesis can be as deep as the
evidence allows; the *email* does not repeat it. The packet must answer **"What
research was intentionally omitted from the email, and why?"** — a strong answer
shows you know the difference between research and communication.

---

## A. Verified trigger
One current, externally observable trigger: new service · new product · seasonal
campaign · new location · updated brand positioning · repeated promotion of a
specific offer · event · booking push · portfolio launch · hiring that signals
expansion · current campaign page · recent public founder statement.

Prefer evidence from the **last 45 days**. If older, explain why it stays
commercially current. **Not** valid triggers: the business exists · it offers
premium services · it has an Instagram account · its website could look better.

## B. Apparent business objective
The most likely immediate objective: increase consultations for one service · fill
seasonal bookings · explain a differentiated offer · launch a product · convert
social attention into inquiries · present a higher-end brand position · support a
new location · improve the campaign→purchase transition.

Mark inferences explicitly: `Inference based on: [specific evidence]`. Never
present an inference as confirmed fact.

## C. Existing customer journey
Map the **real** path (inspect the actual pages and the mobile flow — do not
assume):
`Discovery source → destination → information path → CTA → booking/purchase`
e.g. `Instagram Reel → homepage → full treatment menu → contact form` or
`Product post → general Shopify collection → product page → checkout`.

## D. Verified friction or opportunity
Exactly one primary issue, stated as observable evidence. Allowed forms: campaign
message and destination page mismatch · a promoted offer buried inside a general
site · strong visual identity ending at a generic booking UI · differentiation
stated but not visually demonstrated · too many decisions before the intended CTA
· a high-value service with no focused explanation path · a new product with no
connected launch story · social activity with no owned campaign destination · an
unclear primary CTA for the promoted offer.

**Not** allowed: "the website is bad / the design is outdated / branding is weak /
they need better content." State what is observable.

**Site-wide contradiction check (mandatory before claiming any absence).** A
homepage CTA shortcut is not proof the rest of the site lacks something. Before
claiming/implying a business lacks a **dedicated page · treatment info · product
info · a booking path · a campaign · video · social content · a service explanation
· a specific CTA**, inspect the **homepage, the relevant service/product pages, the
navigation, the sitemap (when available), the booking destination, current campaign
pages, and the official social profiles.** If contradictory evidence exists, the
real issue is usually **fragmentation** (promotion, fuller explanation, and booking
action in separate experiences), not absence — revise the thesis accordingly. If you
cannot resolve the contradiction, **do not draft.** Record findings in section I.

## E. MOTIONX intervention hypothesis
Exactly **one** intervention: Product Film · Motion-First Website · Product Launch
Page · Film + Website Launch System · Campaign Content Add-On. Explain: (1) what
would be created, (2) what message it clarifies, (3) what customer action it
supports, (4) why it fits better than the other MOTIONX services. No revenue or
conversion promises.

## F. Why now
One concrete timely reason: the service is currently promoted · a seasonal booking
window is approaching · the launch is active · the business is expanding · recent
content signals strategic emphasis · attention exists but no focused destination.
"No reason not to contact them" is not a reason.

## G. Decision-maker relevance
Name · role · evidence the person influences marketing/ownership/brand/growth ·
why the proposed issue falls inside their likely responsibility. Do not contact a
generic employee just because their email is easier to find. (See the
decision-maker fit gate in SKILL.md Step 6b.)

## H. Disconfirming evidence
Actively search for reasons NOT to contact: they recently launched an excellent
focused campaign page · they already work with a creative agency · the promoted
offer is no longer active · the journey is already cohesive · the decision-maker is
wrong · budget/scale looks incompatible · MOTIONX has no credible relevant
example. **If disconfirming evidence outweighs the thesis, reject the lead.**

## I. Contradictory or qualifying evidence found elsewhere on the official site
**Mandatory section.** Record what the site-wide contradiction check (section D)
surfaced: anything on the homepage / service or product pages / nav / sitemap /
booking destination / campaign pages / official socials that **qualifies or
contradicts** the apparent friction. State whether it changed the thesis (e.g. from
"no page for the treatment" → "the promo, the explanation, and the booking action
are fragmented across separate experiences"). If a contradiction remains unresolved,
do not draft.

## J. Commercial-readiness
Confirm the customer action the concept implies is **currently possible**: product
availability, booking availability, active location, current service status, a live
offer, a valid checkout/inquiry path. Answer: **"Does the creative idea depend on an
action the customer cannot currently complete?"** If yes → reframe (brand/launch
film, homepage/social usage), hold, or reject. Never pitch driving customers to an
action that isn't available (e.g. a "purchasable collection page" when the SKUs read
Unavailable) unless availability is reverified.

## Source-confidence hierarchy (every factual claim)
- **Tier 1 — primary official:** official website · service/product page · official
  social account · official founder profile · official press release · booking page
  · campaign/event page.
- **Tier 2 — reliable corroborating:** reputable local publication · professional
  association profile · verified business listing · established industry
  publication · public founder interview.
- **Tier 3 — discovery-only:** aggregators · generic directories · search snippets
  · unverified blogs · scraped profile sites. May help discover a lead but must
  **not** support the central claim without corroboration.

Requirements:
- ≥3 meaningful sources when available; ≥1 Tier 1.
- The **trigger and primary thesis must be supported by Tier 1 evidence.**
- Every factual claim records its exact URL, source tier, and access date.
- Never rely solely on a search-result snippet.
