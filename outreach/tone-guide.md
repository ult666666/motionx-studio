# MOTIONX outreach — tone guide

Write as **Charles Dai**, founder of MOTIONX, to one person at one business he
actually studied. Every email is one-to-one. If a sentence could be sent
unchanged to ten businesses, it fails.

## Voice
Observant · sincere · direct · calm · curious · professional but not corporate ·
confident without exaggeration · human, not "optimized." First-person singular
where natural. Short enough to read on a phone.

## Three-layer model (every email — fails on recognition without interpretation)
- **Layer 1 — Recognition:** one specific verified observation (why Charles
  noticed *this* business). Not generic praise.
- **Layer 2 — Interpretation (most important):** a thoughtful inference about the
  campaign / customer path / commercial opportunity — judgment, not just research.
- **Layer 3 — Relevant idea:** one restrained MOTIONX concept tied directly to
  Layer 2.

> Insufficient: "I saw your recent post about your facial membership."
> Better: "Your recent posts are putting the facial membership at the center of
> the brand, but the linked site still takes visitors into the full treatment menu."

## Shape (vary it — never a fixed template)
Each email must achieve, without becoming a visible template:
1. Specific recognition (Layer 1).
2. Relevant interpretation (Layer 2).
3. One concept (Layer 3).
4. A brief, honest reason MOTIONX fits.
5. One low-friction, permission-based question.

**Research depth ≠ email length (v2.5 standard; numbers updated 2026-07-02 to match
the engine).** The packet may be detailed; the email must not repeat the thesis.
Prefer **90–125 words**; acceptable **75–140 words** (the engine enforces hard 75–140)
when the thesis genuinely needs it — and explain why in the packet when outside the
preferred range. **Never lengthen an email to prove research was performed.** Never add filler,
split natural sentences, drop necessary context, or force identical paragraph rhythm
to hit a number. The higher-priority gates always win: specificity, accuracy,
natural founder voice, non-transferability, commercial relevance, respectful
framing. Use only business-relevant public information; don't expose research or
sound intrusive.

**Diagnosis limit.** At most **one sentence** describes friction or an unrealized
opportunity. **At least 70%** of the email concerns the idea, its relevance, and the
easy next step — not criticism or diagnosis.

**Vary the structure.** Don't let every email become "I noticed X → but Y →
MOTIONX can Z → want details?". A paragraph-rhythm check (SKILL.md Step 7b) rejects
drafts that repeat the structure of the last ten drafted messages; let the thesis
shape the email.

## Respectful-friction rule
Never lead by telling a business what is wrong with its site or marketing. Use:
`what's already working → the focused opportunity it creates → one possible MOTIONX
extension`. Frame the idea as a **hypothesis**: "I think there may be an opportunity
to…", "I could see this working as…", "One focused extension might be…", "Those
pieces could work well together as…".

**Banned interpretation phrases** (unless direct, reliable evidence supports the
exact claim): "the one gap is", "your story stops", "visitors lose interest", "you
are losing bookings", "this would convert better", "this tends to hold attention
better", "your page is confusing", "your site is outdated", "your copy is trying
to", "your current content cannot finish the job", plus "you're losing customers",
"your marketing isn't converting", "you need a redesign", "I found several problems".
**No conversion or attention claims without data.** State a measurable technical
error only when verified and genuinely relevant.

## Plain-language gate (no over-written prose)
Reject or simplify any sentence that: sounds like advertising copy inside a sales
email; uses an abstract creative metaphor to demonstrate intelligence; contains
**multiple em dashes**; takes more than one read to understand; sounds like a
portfolio manifesto; or makes the sender the hero instead of the prospect's product.
**At most one restrained visual phrase per email.** Prefer concrete description —
"water, skin, movement, and the bottle in a real setting" — over abstract claims
about what motion "finishes". (Failing examples from the first drafts: "the copy is
reaching for a feeling", "motion does what a still can't quite finish", "so that
escape actually moves".)

## Greeting (must match what is proven)
Use a first name only when the person is a verified decision-maker AND the email is
publicly tied to them or the mailbox reasonably reaches them AND the evidence is
current. For a **general mailbox** (`info@`, `hello@`, `contact@`, `experience@`)
where the named person's control is uncertain, greet **`Hi [Brand] team,`** or
**`Hi [Name] and team,`**. Do not put a specific first name on a general inbox
unless the packet explains why.

## The single offer to mention (pick one, don't paste the menu)
- Product Film — $399
- Motion-First Website — $699
- Film + Website Launch System — $999
- Campaign Content Add-On — $190

**Price is optional.** Include it when it meaningfully qualifies a small-business
lead or removes uncertainty; omit it when the concept or recipient is more sensitive
and the number would distract from the idea. **The review packet must say why price
was included or omitted.** Mention only one offer.

**Price phrasing — use the pilot-rate form:** "my pilot rate for this is $399" /
"my pilot rate for a page like this is $699" / "I'm opening a few pilot projects at
$399". **Do NOT** write "a film runs around $399", "building that page is exactly
what we do", "our prices start at", or any package list. Don't force pricing or both
MOTIONX links into an email. Prefer one relevant link (site, /pilot, or the single
most relevant case study).

## Subject line (stricter subject editor)
**2–5 words**, truthful, naming the actual idea — not a generic agency pitch. Good
directions: `Clear + Brilliant flow`, `Ibiza in motion`, `[Product] launch page`,
`[Offer] booking path`. **Check the last ten subjects and reject any recurring
template pattern** — especially the **`A [deliverable] for [product]`** shape
(`A page for…`, `A short film for…`). Also reject: `Creative idea for…`, `A concept
for…`, Re:, Fwd:, Urgent, Quick question, Partnership proposal/opportunity, Growth
opportunity, Website idea, Increase bookings, Collaboration, Free concept, Huge
Opportunity, Guaranteed Results, Last Chance, Free, ALL CAPS, or fake
personalization. Don't just insert the company name into a generic phrase. Run the
non-transferability test on the subject too.

## Human voice (v2.5) — sound like Charles, not a copywriter
A longer email is not a more human one. Write like a young founder who actually looked
at the business and had **one genuine thought**, not like an agency demonstrating skill.
- **One real reaction, then the idea.** Lead with what you actually noticed or felt
  ("The part that caught my eye…", "I kept thinking…", "I had to read it twice…"), then
  why it made you think of the idea. Personalized *information* (a product name) is not
  enough — show personalized *thought*. Brand-noun test: if removing the company/product
  names leaves an email you could send to any competitor, rewrite with reasoning.
- **Plain words.** Prefer show / make / film / page / product / booking / collection /
  moment. Go easy on *visual identity, cinematic destination, carry the story,
  recognizably [Brand], focused destination, motion language, elevate, amplify, unlock,
  transform* — concentration of these reads as AI.
- **Loosen up.** Vary sentence length and paragraph count; don't force the same
  observation→idea→credibility→CTA shape every time. One intentionally short sentence is
  good. At most one em dash, at most one three-item list, no sentence you couldn't say
  out loud in one breath. Go easy on could/would/might (one or two, not five).
- **Don't pre-scope the project.** One or two concrete, producible details — not a shot
  list, channel plan, or finished concept. Cold email starts a conversation; it doesn't
  deliver the work.
- **Credibility, lightly.** The signature already says MOTIONX. Introduce yourself in a
  way that fits the email ("I'm Charles, founder of MOTIONX" / "I make short product
  films through MOTIONX") and don't repeat the same line across the batch.
- **Price** stays optional (Film $399 · Site $699 · System $999 · Add-on $190); include
  it for small independents and simple offers, omit for PR/design-lead/specialized
  studios where a low pilot number reads small — and record why in the packet.

## Batch diversity & humanization (v2.4)
A run of emails must not read as one template with the nouns swapped. Across an
authorized batch, `batch_preflight()` enforces (and the editor should pre-empt):
- **Subjects:** vary the shape. ≤25% may end "on film", ≤25% "in motion", ≤25% use
  "page"; no exact duplicate; no templated skeleton (e.g. "One X for Y") more than 4×.
  Clarity beats novelty — never an awkward subject just to differ.
- **CTAs:** rotate naturally across categories (written direction · short conversation
  · scope outline · interest check · timing check); no exact CTA more than 4× in 20.
- **MOTIONX positioning:** vary the opener ("I run MOTIONX" / "I lead MOTIONX" /
  "MOTIONX is my…" / "At MOTIONX I…" / "I'm Charles, and MOTIONX…") and match it to the
  offer AND the prospect type. Never call an architecture/lighting, design-studio,
  photography, or other service-brand film a "product film" — say *brand/capability
  film*. Don't advertise "launch systems" in a single Product Film pitch.
- **Filler phrases:** the same crutch across unrelated businesses reads automated. Avoid
  recurring "a natural fit", "a good moment", "a natural moment", "could carry", "give
  it a home". (Tracked at batch level — concentration >25% fails.)
- **Respect & honesty:** never judge their current site/template, never infer business
  maturity/occupancy/demand from a promotion, and never propose creating something they
  already have — improve or extend it instead. Re-verify time-sensitive premises; don't
  pitch a stale/ended seasonal launch as if current.

## CTA — permission, not pressure
Good: "Would a brief written direction be useful?" / "Open to a short conversation
about it?" / "Would it help if I outlined the scope?" / "Is the timing right on your
side?" Never demand a call, calendar booking, proposal review, contract, or deposit in
a first email — and never promise free/speculative production (sketch, mockup, "rough
visual direction").

## Language blacklist (avoid unless truly unavoidable)
I hope this email finds you well · I came across · I wanted to reach out · huge
opportunity · unlock · elevate · revolutionize · game-changing · synergy ·
leverage · cutting-edge · transform your brand · take your business to the next
level · seamless · captivating · high-converting · guaranteed · "63% of
consumers" · in today's digital landscape.

Do **not** insert fake typos to seem human. Human = specific thinking, not errors.

## Formatting limits
Plain text or minimal email-safe formatting. **1 link preferred, 2 only when
genuinely necessary** (site, /pilot, or the single most relevant case study);
exactly 1 recipient, no BCC, no CC (unless truly necessary), no attachment, no
tracking pixel, no link shortener, no heavy HTML, no autoplay video, no large
images. **Do NOT include the studio-reel thumbnail in initial prospect drafts**
unless Charles later approves — the first message prioritizes relevance over
showing every asset.

## Signature — insert the established motionX signature (v2.6.1, updated 2026-06-24)
**Type the motionX signature into the body after the email content**, then the
compliance footer. API-created drafts do not reliably receive Gmail's auto-applied
signature, so insert the plain-text mirror of `outreach/signature.html` — the exact
block rendered by `preflight.py:render_signature()` (the ONLY approved signature; do
not improvise):

```
--
Charles Dai
Founder, MotionX
Product Films · Motion-First Websites · Launch Systems
Website: https://madebymotionx.com
Email Address: charles@madebymotionx.com
```

The v2.6.1 block keeps the `--` delimiter, the labelled `Website:` / `Email Address:`
lines, and the founder address `charles@madebymotionx.com`. It has **no** "Available
Worldwide" line and **no** "Based in" location — both are now banned tokens, so
`check_signature()` fails `SIGNATURE_DRIFT` on them (and on the public alias
`hello@madebymotionx.com`). The `Website:` line keeps the full `https://` scheme. Pass
the website link via an `htmlBody` https anchor (`href="https://madebymotionx.com"`,
display text "madebymotionx.com") — Gmail then wraps it as `google.com/url?q=https://…`,
its normal tracking redirect (fine only inside the html anchor); a bare plaintext URL
downgrades to `http://`, and a wrapper left visible in plaintext is the batch20/21
defect, so always supply the `htmlBody` anchor. If Charles's Gmail also auto-inserts
"motionX" on send, opening an existing draft and pressing Send does NOT re-insert it,
so this does not duplicate (only re-composing would). The connector cannot read/set
the Gmail signature, so keep it and `signature.html` in sync. (Charles owns the final
outward-facing signature identity — confirm any change to it before an outreach run.)
